\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Conclusion}

Working with synthetic data allowed us to build and test a complete pipeline for the market rent
estimate before the real data arrived. We built a listing-level master table, protected it against
leakage by selecting variables by role, and trained models on held-out buildings. Gradient boosting
provides a working baseline that cuts the error of a simple benchmark by \errorReduction{}. Because
the synthetic data records the true market rent, we could also show that the remaining error is not
noise in the listings but missing information about each building. Finally, we found that
concession types are already recorded, so the work in concession extraction lies in recovering their
value. However, these results cannot be considered representative of actual rental market
conditions, and they need to be confirmed on real data. Accolade has now delivered the real Dwellsy
listings, and we are moving our pipeline over to them.

\subsection{Future Work}

Our next steps build directly on these results:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Comparable-property selection.} Since the remaining error reflects missing information
  about each building, we will build the comparable-property baseline described in Accolade's plan,
  using similarity in location, quality, size, vintage, and amenities, and compare our models
  against it.
  \item \textbf{Combined variables.} We will merge the numerical and categorical variables under the
  same exclusion rules, starting with property class, and train with cross-validation grouped by
  property.
  \item \textbf{Additional models.} Beyond gradient boosting, we plan to evaluate Random Forest,
  XGBoost, and LightGBM, consider stacking based on their performance, and tune hyperparameters with
  Optuna~\autocite{akiba2019optuna}.
  \item \textbf{Ranges and explanations.} Every estimate needs a range and its reasons, so we will add
  quantile or conformal prediction intervals~\autocite{angelopoulos2023gentle}, report their coverage,
  and explain predictions with SHAP values~\autocite{lundberg2017unified}. Accolade has also asked for
  an interpretable model, which we will weigh against predictive performance.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation.} We will report the median absolute percentage error by unit type and
  market, interval coverage, and performance on a later time period.
  \item \textbf{Phase 2.} We will continue with concession extraction and the demand-based price
  recommendation, for which we are considering survival models such as Random Survival
  Forests~\autocite{ishwaran2008random} and DeepSurv~\autocite{katzman2018deepsurv}.
  \item \textbf{Real data.} With the real Dwellsy data now delivered, our immediate priority is to rerun
  the full pipeline on it, starting with Chicago.
\end{itemize}
